NO FREE LUNCH // ENGINEERING SCOPE & PHYSICAL TRADE-OFFS ON THIS PAGE

No Free Lunch in Silicon — 8.0 ns Cut-Through Speed vs. Mode-D Power-Trace Armor

Every silicon number on this page comes with an explicit physical trade-off you can see in the simulator above:

1. Mode-D WDDL Halves Speed & Uses 4x Energy

Standard Mode-C runs at full 128 Gbps wire speed using 35.0 mW (16nm FPGA) or 3.728 mW (7nm ASIC). Turning on Mode-D WDDL dual-rail side-channel armor (max |t| = 0.0) pre-charges every wire every other beat—cutting throughput by 50% and using >= 4x more energy per bit.

2. 16nm FPGA vs. 7nm ASIC Clock Headroom

On a 16nm AMD xcvu47p FPGA, wire routing delay (1.935 ns) limits the core to 338.8 MHz (+1.048 ns slack at 250 MHz). Reaching 693 MHz (1.444 ns path) requires synthesizing the 27,877 standard cells into 7nm CMOS.

3. Read-Scrubbing Needs the 341-LUT ARID Shim

Blocking a bad write only takes wiring wstrb = 0x0000 in the 1,585-LUT core. Blocking a bad read without hanging the CPU's transaction tracker requires the extra 341-LUT Per-ARID shim to return a clean zeroed cacheline.
